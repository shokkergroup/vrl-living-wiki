# The 25-genre playbook

Ranked on two separate axes, because they are not the same thing:

- **NEED (1–5)** — how much value is trapped in the archive. How buried is it,
  how much recurring lore is going to waste, how big is the watch-hour upside.
- **WANT (1–5)** — how likely they are to actually buy. Is there a budget, is
  there a single decision-maker, and do they already think about their archive
  as an asset.

**High need with low want is a trap.** A hobbyist with 800 buried hours and no
revenue needs this desperately and will never pay for it. Sort by the pair.

---

## How to use this

**Do not work the list.** Pick **two** verticals and own them completely.

The second build in a vertical is dramatically faster than the first — shared
taxonomy, shared signature mechanics, shared clip signals, shared outreach
language. Two verticals done deeply beats ten done once, and it produces
referrals inside a community that already talks to itself.

Every gameplan below names a **signature fan mechanic**. That is the thing that
would look absurd on any other channel — the no-reskin test made concrete. If
you build a genre and cannot name that mechanic, you have built a template.

---

## The tiers

| Tier | Genres | Move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1 · Beachhead** | 1–6 | Go now. Proven, or near-certain. |
| **2 · Strong** | 7–14 | Real money, slightly harder route in. |
| **3 · Patient** | 15–20 | Genuine need, slower or fuzzier buyer. |
| **4 · Opportunistic** | 21–25 | Take them if they come to you. |

# TIER 1 · BEACHHEAD

## 1. Sim racing & esports leagues — Need 5 · Want 5

**Why now:** Proven with VRL. Seasons, standings, and results are a perfect
ledger fit, there is no RSS feed so no competitor can reach them, and leagues
have a single owner who decides in one conversation.

**World:** Race control — timing tower, stewards' room, broadcast archive.

**Native objects:** races, seasons, drivers, teams, tracks, results, incidents,
penalties, booth calls, broadcast eras.

**Signature fan mechanic — The Penalty Box.** Every stewards' decision in league
history, playable at the exact second, with the radio call and the booth
reaction attached. Fans argue about these for years and there is currently
nowhere to settle it. Pair with **Championship Paths** — what had to happen for
the title to swing.

**Creator tool:** race recap packet the moment the broadcast ends; sponsor
moment finder for renewal decks.

**Watch-time hook:** Race Night Mode — moment toasts fire live while a fan
rewatches a full broadcast. Full-length views, the highest-value kind.

**Buyer & route in:** League owner or sanctioning body. **Sell the sanctioning
body, not the club** — one relationship, many archives.

**Typical:** Tier 2–3, band B2–B3.

**Pitch:** *"Every restart, every penalty, every title fight, settled with the
tape."*

---

## 2. Wrestling history & talk — Need 5 · Want 5

**Why now:** Enormous recurring lore, an argumentative fanbase that already pays
for niche wrestling media, and back catalogs measured in years.

**World:** The territory office and the tape library.

**Native objects:** wrestlers, promotions, territories, matches, angles, titles,
promoters, towns, eras, takes.

**Signature fan mechanic — The Kayfabe Line.** Separate what the show presents
as *documented history* from what it presents as *storyline or worked*. That is
the evidence-tier system applied to the one subject where the distinction is the
whole art form. Nobody else can do this and wrestling fans will lose their minds
over it. Pair with the **Territory Map** — click a town, hear everything the
show ever said about it.

**Creator tool:** named-wrestler research packet before recording; prior-take
lookup so callbacks are intentional instead of accidental repeats.

**Watch-time hook:** the **"Called It" docket** — every prediction the host made,
resolved against what actually happened, each one playable.

**Buyer & route in:** Host or small production team. Wrestling media people know
each other; one good build travels.

**Typical:** Tier 3, band B2–B4.

**Pitch:** *"Your show has been writing wrestling history for years. Nobody can
search it."*

## 3. True crime — Need 5 · Want 4

**Why now:** The most entity-dense genre there is, an audience famous for
obsessive detail, and real money in the category. The subject matter *demands*
exactly the evidence discipline nobody else has — refusal to infer is a feature
here, not overhead.

**World:** The case room. Evidence board, not a spooky aesthetic.

**Native objects:** cases, victims, suspects, agencies, jurisdictions, dates,
locations, claims, theories, status changes, sources.

**Signature fan mechanic — The Correction Wall.** A public, append-only record of
what the show got wrong and fixed. In true crime that is *reputation armor* —
the single most valuable thing a responsible host can display, and the thing
that separates them from the genre's worst actors. Pair with the **Open
Questions Board**: what remains genuinely unresolved, stated as unresolved.

**Creator tool:** development alerts when a case status changes, so the host
knows to revisit; prior-coverage lookup before recording a follow-up.

**Watch-time hook:** **Case Timeline** — play the show's coverage chronologically
as the case actually developed, across years of episodes.

**Buyer & route in:** Host or small network. Lead with liability and credibility,
not with features.

**Typical:** Tier 3–4, band B2–B3.

**Pitch:** *"Every claim, sourced. Every correction, public. Every unknown, still
unknown."*

⚠️ Screen the prospect. Decline shows that speculate about living suspects or
name private individuals loosely. The evidence system cannot protect a host who
does not want to be protected.

## 4. Actual play & TTRPG campaigns — Need 5 · Want 4

**Why now:** Hundreds of hours of serialized story, a canon-obsessed fanbase,
and effectively zero tooling in the entire category.

**World:** The campaign journal — a DM's binder, not a generic wiki.

**Native objects:** campaigns, arcs, sessions, player characters, NPCs, items,
locations, deaths, rolls, running jokes.

**Signature fan mechanic — Character Chronicle.** One character's entire arc
across 300 hours, rendered as a playable path from first appearance to last.
Pair with the **Dice Ledger** — every natural 20 and natural 1 in campaign
history, playable. Fans will share these relentlessly.

**Creator tool:** continuity packet before a session — *"what does the party
actually know about this NPC?"* — which is a real, weekly, painful problem for
every GM running a long campaign.

**Watch-time hook:** **The Catch-Up Path.** New viewers are genuinely terrified
of a 300-hour campaign, and that fear is the single biggest growth ceiling in
this genre. A curated onboarding route that makes the back catalog approachable
converts intimidated visitors into binge viewers. **This is the highest-value
mechanic in the entire playbook.**

**Buyer & route in:** The cast, usually one producer. Communities are tight and
loud; one build gets seen.

**Typical:** Tier 3, band B3–B4.

**Pitch:** *"Three hundred hours is a wall for new fans. Make it a door."*

## 5. Faith & sermon archives — Need 5 · Want 4

**Why now:** Decades of video, an institution with budget and continuity, a
congregation that genuinely searches for what was said about a topic or passage,
and **literally no competitor serving them.**

**World:** The study. Warm, quiet, reverent. Resist cleverness entirely.

**Native objects:** series, messages, passages, topics, speakers, dates,
ministries, life events.

**Signature fan mechanic — The Scripture Index.** Click any verse and get every
time it was ever preached, playable at the second. Simple, obvious, and nobody
has built it.

**Creator tool:** prior-message lookup before writing; series gap analysis
showing what has not been addressed in years.

**Watch-time hook:** **The Care Path.** Topic routes for someone going through
grief, a new marriage, addiction, a job loss, a death. Someone finds this at 2am
in real need and watches four messages. It is pastoral rather than clever, and
it is the most genuinely useful thing in this document.

**Buyer & route in:** Executive pastor or media director. Budget is annual and
approved by committee — slower, but sticky and not price-sensitive.

**Typical:** Tier 2–3, band B3–B5. Archives here are enormous.

**Pitch:** *"Thirty years of teaching, findable by anyone who needs it tonight."*

---

## 6. Horror, film & watchalong — Need 4 · Want 5

**Why now:** Proven with WWAM. Recurring bits, characters, and verdicts give
enormous native structure, and the audience is already participatory.

**World:** The after-hours video store / evidence room.

**Native objects:** films, franchises, hosts, guests, recurring characters,
running bits, verdicts, reactions, watchalong timing.

**Signature fan mechanic — Franchise Autopsy.** Every film in a franchise ranked
by the show's *own evolving verdict over years*, with the receipts showing where
they changed their mind. Pair with **Bit Bloodlines** — a running joke's entire
lineage from first utterance forward.

**Creator tool:** character-performance queue; cold-open factory built from
recurring bits.

**Watch-time hook:** **Watchalong Companion** — sync the archive alongside the
actual film. Produces full-length views.

**Buyer & route in:** Host. Horror communities are extremely online and share
hard.

**Typical:** Tier 3–4, band B2–B3.

**Pitch:** *"Your running jokes have a bloodline. Show it."*

---

## 6b. Quiz, trivia & game show archives — Need 4 · Want 4

**Added after auditing real subscription data.** This genre was missing from the
original list and it should not have been — it is arguably the **lowest-effort
vertical in the entire playbook.**

**Why now:** Format repetition is total. The same structure every episode means
distillation is nearly mechanical, per-source cost stays low, and most builds
land at E1–E2. The channels are small, numerous, and reachable, and the
*archive-native* ones — channels that exist purely to republish classic episodes
— have already decided the back catalog is the product. You are not selling them
the premise, only the execution.

**World:** The studio floor, the buzzer, the board.

**Native objects:** questions, answers, categories, difficulty, episodes,
contestants, hosts, formats, air dates, prizes, rule changes.

**Signature fan mechanic — The Question Bank.** Every question ever asked,
searchable by category, difficulty, and answer, each one playable at the exact
second it was read. That is the purest **Index** shape in the whole system and
nobody in the genre has built it. Layer a personal score-tracker on top and it
becomes a returning weekly ritual — a watch-time engine that runs itself.

For classic game show archives, swap in **The Contestant Registry** (one page per
contestant, every appearance) and **The Format Timeline** (how a show's rules
changed across eras).

**Creator tool:** duplicate-question detection before recording, category gap
analysis, and difficulty balancing across an episode.

**Watch-time hook:** category paths and difficulty ladders. A viewer who keeps
missing one category comes back to chase it — deliberate, repeated,
intent-driven plays.

**Buyer & route in:** Usually a solo operator, often under 60K subscribers, who
answers a DM. Archive-native channels are the softest conversation available.

**Typical:** Tier 1–2, band B1–B2. Fast.

**Pitch:** *"Every question you've ever asked, searchable, playable, and scored."*

# TIER 2 · STRONG

## 7. Radio show archives — Need 5 · Want 4

**Why now:** Decades of bits, characters, callers, and cast changes sitting on
hard drives and unlisted uploads. **And you have 27 years of relationships in
this world — this is likely the fastest first sale available to you.**

**World:** The studio. The board, the mic flags, the request line.

**Native objects:** bits, characters, callers, segments, guests, co-hosts, eras,
station moves.

**Signature fan mechanic — The Caller Hall of Fame.** Recurring callers get their
own pages, with every appearance playable. Radio audiences have deep affection
for these people and nobody has ever indexed them. Pair with **The Era Board** —
cast changes across decades, so a listener can jump to "the years I listened."

**Creator tool:** best-of assembly for a station anniversary or a departure;
bit-revival queue.

**Watch-time hook:** era paths. A listener who moved away in 2011 comes back and
binges their own era.

**Buyer & route in:** Show host, program director, or the station group. Your
network is the route.

**Typical:** Tier 3, band B3–B5.

**Pitch:** *"Twenty years of the show, and the only copy is in people's heads."*

---

## 8. Local & school sports broadcasts — Need 5 · Want 4

**Why now:** Boosters, parents, and alumni are deeply motivated and there is no
alternative product at any price.

**World:** The trophy case and the field house.

**Native objects:** seasons, teams, players, games, plays, coaches, rivalries,
venues, records.

**Signature fan mechanic — Player Pages.** Every play a kid ever made, forever,
on one page with their name on it. That is an heirloom, not a feature.

**Creator tool — the Recruiting Export.** A player's highlight set with
timestamps and source links, exported for college coaches. **Parents will fund
the entire build for this alone.**

**Watch-time hook:** senior night, rivalry history, and the comeback board —
all of which resurrect broadcasts nobody has opened in a decade.

**Buyer & route in:** Athletic director, booster club, or the broadcast
volunteer who already films every game. Booster clubs have budgets and love
spending on legacy.

**Typical:** Tier 2–3, band B2–B4.

**Pitch:** *"Every kid who ever played here gets a page."*

---

## 9. Combat sports analysis — Need 4 · Want 4

**Why now:** Fight-by-fight archives with dense entity structure, and analysts
whose entire reputation rests on their record.

**World:** The gym and the fight card.

**Native objects:** fighters, fights, cards, techniques, camps, weight classes,
finishes, judges.

**Signature fan mechanic — The Prediction Ledger.** Every pick the analyst ever
made, resolved against the result, with the reasoning playable. Analysts live
and die on this and most are quietly terrified of it — which is exactly why the
ones who agree are worth working with.

**Creator tool:** pre-fight research packet on both fighters; technique
breakdown index.

**Watch-time hook:** fighter pages that resurface old breakdowns the week a
fighter is booked again. Timed resurrection is enormous here.

**Buyer & route in:** Analyst or small media outfit.

**Typical:** Tier 3, band B2–B3.

**Pitch:** *"Your record, in public, with the tape."*

---

## 10. Music: live shows, tours & taper archives — Need 5 · Want 3

**Why now:** The most archival fanbase on earth. Jam band, metal, and touring
communities already maintain setlist databases by hand.

**World:** The setlist book.

**Native objects:** shows, songs, venues, tours, lineups, guests, segues, debuts.

**Signature fan mechanic — Song History.** Every performance of one song across
every show, playable, with the version fans argue is definitive marked and
sourced. Pair with **Debut & Bustout tracking** — first time played, and longest
gap since last played. These communities *already* track this manually.

**Creator tool:** anniversary posts that write themselves; archival release
sequencing.

**Watch-time hook:** tour paths and venue pages. A fan who was at one show
watches six.

**Buyer & route in:** Band management, an archival label, or the taper community
itself. **Want is the weak axis** — the fans are rabid but the buyer is often
unclear. Find the one with rights.

**Typical:** Tier 3–4, band B4–B5.

**Pitch:** *"Every version of every song, in one place, playable."*

---

## 11. Outdoors: hunting, fishing & powersports — Need 4 · Want 4

**Why now:** Big archives, deep sponsor money, and a gear-obsessed audience.

**World:** The map and the camp.

**Native objects:** trips, locations, species, gear, seasons, conditions,
techniques, tags.

**Signature fan mechanic — The Gear Index.** Every product ever used, on camera,
with every mention playable. This is where the money is: **a sponsor may fund
the entire build**, because it turns a one-time read into a permanent,
searchable, linkable asset.

**Creator tool:** sponsor renewal deck generated from the index; seasonal
resurfacing before each opener.

**Watch-time hook:** species and location pages that spike hard every season.
Genuinely seasonal traffic that resurrects old uploads on a schedule.

**Buyer & route in:** Creator, or their sponsor directly. **Selling the sponsor
is the unusual move here and it works.**

**Typical:** Tier 3, band B2–B3.

**Pitch:** *"Your sponsor's read stops being 40 seconds and starts being a
permanent page."*

## 12. Grassroots motorsport: dirt, drag & short track — Need 5 · Want 3

**Why now:** Decades of local broadcasts, fiercely loyal regional communities,
and archives nobody has ever indexed.

**World:** The pit gate and the tower.

**Native objects:** races, classes, cars, numbers, drivers, tracks, seasons,
purses, wrecks.

**Signature fan mechanic — The Car Number Registry.** Track a car number across
decades and multiple owners and drivers. In short-track culture a number *is* an
identity, inherited and fought over, and nobody has ever mapped it.

**Creator tool:** season recap packets; track-anniversary features.

**Watch-time hook:** track pages and class histories, plus the wreck reel
handled with taste.

**Buyer & route in:** Track promoter or the local broadcaster. Promoters are
reachable and decide fast; budgets are modest.

**Typical:** Tier 2, band B3–B4.

**Pitch:** *"That number has been running here since 1994. Show the whole
lineage."*

---

## 13. Comedy podcast networks — Need 4 · Want 4

**Why now:** Callback-dense, multi-show, and the humor *depends* on shared
history that new listeners cannot access.

**World:** The clubhouse.

**Native objects:** bits, running jokes, guests, callbacks, feuds, eras,
crossovers.

**Signature fan mechanic — Callback Trails.** A joke's entire lineage across
multiple shows in the network, playable in order. Cross-show is the unlock:
individual shows are searchable, a *network's* shared mythology is not.

**Creator tool:** bit-revival queue; crossover planning; guest history before a
return appearance.

**Watch-time hook:** "explain this joke" routes for new listeners, which is the
exact conversion problem every comedy network has.

**Buyer & route in:** Network owner. One sale, many shows — the best economics
in the playbook.

**Typical:** Tier 4, band B4–B5, multi-feed add-ons.

**Pitch:** *"The inside jokes are the moat. Right now nobody new can get in."*

---

## 14. Conference & talk archives — Need 4 · Want 4

**Why now:** Ten-plus years of talks, an organization with a budget, and an
archive that currently exists as an unwatched playlist.

**World:** The program and the hallway track.

**Native objects:** events, years, speakers, sessions, topics, tracks.

**Signature fan mechanic — Idea Lineage.** Trace a concept across a decade of
talks: who introduced it, who pushed back, what it became. Pair with **Speaker
Trajectory** — one speaker's evolution across every year they appeared.

**Creator tool:** program planning gap analysis; speaker packets; sponsor recaps.

**Watch-time hook:** "the talk that started it" resurfacing, plus pre-event
paths that reactivate the whole archive every year on schedule.

**Buyer & route in:** Conference organizer or the parent association. Annual
budget cycle — time the pitch to it.

**Typical:** Tier 3, band B2–B3.

**Pitch:** *"Ten years of talks, and the only way in is a playlist sorted by
date."*

# TIER 3 · PATIENT

## 15. Civic & municipal video — Need 5 · Want 3

**Why now:** Public record with legal retention requirements, enormous archives,
and citizens and journalists who genuinely need search. Budget exists. The sale
is slow.

**World:** The public record. Plain, institutional, deliberately unglamorous.

**Native objects:** meetings, agenda items, motions, votes, officials,
departments, ordinances, public comment.

**Signature fan mechanic — The Motion Tracker.** Follow one ordinance across
every meeting and year it appeared, playable at each discussion. Pair with
**Vote History** per official.

**Creator tool:** clerk-facing minute assistance; journalist search.

**Watch-time hook:** agenda-item deep links shared in local news and community
groups.

**Buyer & route in:** City clerk, communications director, or IT. Procurement is
real; expect months. **Showcase value:** this genre demands refused answers and
zero inference, which makes it the best possible reference build for the
evidence system.

**Typical:** Tier 2–3, band B4–B5.

**Pitch:** *"The public record, actually searchable by the public."*

## 16. Education & expert evergreen libraries — Need 4 · Want 4

**Why now:** Deep evergreen catalogs whose value decays only because nothing
signals what is still current.

**World:** The curriculum.

**Native objects:** concepts, lessons, examples, mistakes, tools, prerequisites,
updates.

**Signature fan mechanic — The Outdated Advice Alert.** Flag where an older video
conflicts with a newer one, and route the viewer to the current answer. This
builds enormous trust *and* drives watch time to the newer video. Nobody does
it because it requires knowing what the whole archive says.

**Creator tool:** curriculum gap report; "what needs a remake" queue.

**Watch-time hook:** Learning Maps with prerequisites — a viewer arrives for one
answer and walks a path of six.

**Buyer & route in:** Creator, often already selling a course.

**Typical:** Tier 3, band B2–B3.

**Pitch:** *"Your 2019 video still ranks. It's also wrong now. Let's route around
it."*

## 17. Gaming: speedrun & challenge communities — Need 4 · Want 3

**Why now:** Route-obsessed, record-obsessed, and entirely undocumented outside
spreadsheets.

**World:** The route sheet and the leaderboard.

**Native objects:** runs, routes, tricks, categories, records, attempts, resets,
glitches.

**Signature fan mechanic — Route Evolution.** How the optimal route changed over
years, with the exact run where each new trick first appeared, playable. Pair
with **The Trick Index** — discovery moments for every technique.

**Creator tool:** route-change documentation; PB history assembly.

**Watch-time hook:** record-progression paths — watch the world record fall in
sequence across years and runners.

**Buyer & route in:** Individual runner or a community org. Budgets are thin;
community orgs are the better target.

**Typical:** Tier 2–3, band B3–B4.

**Pitch:** *"Every trick has a first run. Nobody can find them."*

## 18. History & documentary channels — Need 4 · Want 3

**Why now:** Fact-dense archives where credibility is the entire product.

**World:** The archive and the master timeline.

**Native objects:** events, people, places, eras, sources, claims.

**Signature fan mechanic — The Master Timeline.** Every episode placed on one
chronological spine, so the channel's whole body of work becomes a single
navigable history. Pair with **Source Trail** — every citation the channel ever
made, indexed.

**Creator tool:** prior-coverage lookup; source reuse tracking.

**Watch-time hook:** era paths and "what happened next" chains.

**Buyer & route in:** Creator, often solo with a researcher.

**Typical:** Tier 3, band B2–B3.

**Pitch:** *"Forty episodes, one timeline, every source."*

---

## 19. Paranormal, UFO & fringe research — Need 4 · Want 3

**Why now:** Case-based and entity-dense, with an audience that argues about
details constantly.

**World:** The case files.

**Native objects:** cases, witnesses, locations, dates, claims, investigators,
documents, status.

**Signature fan mechanic — The Skeptic Lane.** Every case shows the claim *and*
the counter-explanation, each with its own evidence tier and receipt. Handled
honestly this makes a fringe channel dramatically more credible without
softening it — and it is only possible because we grade evidence.

**Creator tool:** claim ledger; document index; recurring-witness tracking.

**Watch-time hook:** case files that pull viewers across many episodes.

**Buyer & route in:** Host. Screen for shows that name and harass private
individuals — decline those.

**Typical:** Tier 3, band B2–B3.

**Pitch:** *"Rank your own evidence before someone else does."*

## 20. Car builds & restoration — Need 4 · Want 4

**Why now:** Serialized, project-based, and the audience arrives with a specific
problem to solve.

**World:** The build sheet and the garage wall.

**Native objects:** builds, parts, problems, fixes, milestones, tools, costs,
suppliers.

**Signature fan mechanic — The Problem Index.** *"How did they fix that?"* is the
number one reason anyone searches this genre, and right now the answer is buried
at minute 34 of episode 61. Indexing fixes by symptom is pure, high-intent watch
time.

**Creator tool:** build recap; parts and supplier index for sponsor decks.

**Watch-time hook:** Build Sheets — one project rendered as a playable path from
teardown to first start.

**Buyer & route in:** Creator or their parts sponsor.

**Typical:** Tier 3, band B2–B3.

**Pitch:** *"Every problem you solved on camera, findable by the symptom."*

# TIER 4 · OPPORTUNISTIC

## 21. Anime & manga lore channels — Need 4 · Want 3

**World:** The archive of a fictional world.

**Signature fan mechanic — The Theory Tracker.** Every theory the channel ever
floated, marked confirmed, debunked, or still open by later canon, with the
original prediction playable. This genre runs entirely on theories and *nobody
keeps score.*

**Watch-time hook:** arc paths and re-watch guides timed to new releases.

**Buyer:** Creator, usually solo. Thin budgets, huge audiences.

**Pitch:** *"You called it in 2021. Prove it."*

---

## 22. AI & technology news — Need 3 · Want 4

**World:** The intelligence desk.

**Signature fan mechanic — The Prediction Scoreboard.** Every forecast, resolved.
Pair with **Model Time Machine** — every claim about one company or model in
chronological order.

**Watch-time hook:** resurfacing an old prediction the week it comes true.

**Buyer:** Creator, often already monetized. Lower need — their content is
timely, so the archive decays fast. Sell the credibility ledger, not the lore.

**Pitch:** *"Timely content ages badly. A scoreboard doesn't."*

---

## 23. Trades, making & woodworking — Need 3 · Want 3

**World:** The shop.

**Signature fan mechanic — The Technique Index,** organized by the mistake it
prevents rather than by the tool used. Searching by failure is how people
actually arrive.

**Watch-time hook:** project paths; tool index for sponsors.

**Buyer:** Creator, often with tool sponsorship.

**Pitch:** *"People search for the mistake, not the technique."*

---

## 24. Sports cards & collectibles — Need 3 · Want 3

**World:** The showcase and the price guide.

**Signature fan mechanic — The Comp Ledger.** Everything the show ever said about
one card's value, in date order, against what happened. Market history as lore.

**Watch-time hook:** card and player pages that spike with market moves.

**Buyer:** Creator or a shop with a channel.

**Pitch:** *"You've been calling this market for years. Show the receipts."*

---

## 25. Finance & investing analysis — Need 3 · Want 4

**World:** The desk.

**Signature fan mechanic — The Call Ledger.** Every position and call, dated and
resolved. Brutal honesty as the differentiator.

**Watch-time hook:** thesis pages that resurface when a name moves.

**Buyer:** Creator, frequently well funded.

⚠️ **Highest compliance risk in the playbook.** Regulatory exposure around
investment advice is real, and a resolved-call ledger is exactly the artifact a
regulator or a plaintiff would read. Require the client's own disclaimers,
never characterize performance ourselves, present the ledger as *what the show
said* rather than as a track record, and get sign-off in writing before launch.
If the prospect is cagey about any of that, walk.

**Pitch:** *"Publish your record before someone else compiles it."*

## The pattern across all 25

Almost every signature mechanic above is one of four shapes. When you enter a
new genre, ask which shape applies:

| Shape | What it is | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Ledger** | Every claim, pick, or prediction, resolved against what happened | Called It docket, Prediction Ledger, Theory Tracker, Call Ledger, Comp Ledger |
| **The Lineage** | One thing traced across the whole archive over time | Bit Bloodlines, Song History, Car Number Registry, Route Evolution, Idea Lineage, Motion Tracker |
| **The Index** | Search by the thing people actually arrive looking for | Problem Index, Gear Index, Scripture Index, Technique Index, Trick Index |
| **The Path** | A curated route that makes an intimidating archive enterable | Catch-Up Path, Care Path, Learning Map, Era Board, Build Sheet |

**The Path shape is the watch-time engine's best friend** — it is the only one
that converts a nervous newcomer into a binge. When in doubt about a new genre,
build a Path first.
