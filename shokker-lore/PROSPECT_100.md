## How this is ranked

You asked for a ranking where an easy, fast build can outrank a bigger payday.
So:

```
SCORE = (VALUE × REACH) ÷ EFFORT
```

- **VALUE (1–10)** — deal size, recurring potential, and reference value to the
  next sale in that vertical.
- **REACH (1–5)** — can you actually get the decision-maker. A 300-subscriber
  league owner answers a DM. A 19M-subscriber network does not.
- **EFFORT (1–5)** — how much of *your* time it takes.

### Effort scale

| | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **E1** | One evening. Under ~100 caption-backed hours, one repeating format, obvious entities. |
| **E2** | A day. ~100–300 hours, clear structure, light identity work. |
| **E3** | Standard build. ~300–800 hours, real identity reconciliation, multiple formats. |
| **E4** | Deep. ~800–2,000 hours, multiple eras, messy canon, heavy review. |
| **E5** | Vault. 2,000+ hours or institutional/legal complexity. |

**The scoring deliberately rewards small.** A 341-subscriber dirt league you can
finish in an evening and reference forever outranks a 19M-subscriber channel
that will never answer you.

# What your subscriptions actually told me

Two findings I did not expect, and one of them changes the playbook.

## 1. You are subscribed to **14 quiz and game show channels**

Quiz Paradise, Quizink, Brainiac's Trivia Vortex, The Game Show Vault, The Quiz
Channel, Top Movies Quiz Show, Quiz me baby one more time, CHAPI QUIZ, YouQuiz,
Game Show Flashback, The Quiz Show, BUZZR, Decathlon Gamer, and The Andy
Griffith Show Facts and Trivia.

**This genre was not in the 25-genre playbook and it should have been.** It is
arguably the single best low-effort vertical available to you:

- **Format repetition is total.** Same structure every episode, so distillation
  is nearly mechanical and per-source token cost stays low.
- **Questions are entities.** A quiz archive is a *question bank* — the purest
  Index shape in the whole system.
- **The build is E1–E2** almost every time.
- **The channels are small and reachable.** Most of the ones you follow are
  under 60K subs.
- **You clearly like this content**, which matters more than it sounds. You will
  build a better world for a genre you actually watch.

The signature mechanic writes itself: **The Question Bank** — every question ever
asked, searchable by category, difficulty, and answer, each playable at the exact
second it was read. Layer a personal score-tracker on top and you have a
returning weekly ritual, which is a watch-time engine that runs itself.

**Recommendation: make quiz/game show your second vertical alongside sim racing.**
Six of them at E1 is a week of evenings and a portfolio nobody else has.

## 2. Archive-native channels are the softest targets you have

Several channels you follow exist **for the sole purpose of republishing old
content**: NASCAR Classics, WWE Vault, WCW, BUZZR, The Game Show Vault, Game
Show Flashback, Movie Central, Disaster Zone Movies.

These operators have already decided the back catalog is the product. You do not
have to sell them the premise — only the execution. That is a fundamentally
shorter conversation than convincing a working creator that their old uploads
matter.

**Start with the small ones** (The Game Show Vault, Game Show Flashback, NASCAR
Classics) where you can reach a person, and use those as the reference for the
corporate ones later.

## 3. Your sim racing cluster is a ready-made beachhead

Fourteen racing channels in your subs, and the small ones — Midwest Days of
Thunder (341), Virtual Grip Network (1.39K), GreeneSportsZone (2.16K), P1
Broadcasting (2.58K), lwsvol (6.67K) — are precisely the profile you described
in your very first message.

**And you already have the case study.** VRL is in your subscriptions. That is
the demo.

The highest-leverage single target in the whole vertical is **Global SimRacing
Channel** — broadcasting since 2013, which means a decade-plus of unindexed
league racing and enough credibility that a build there sells every smaller
league behind it.
