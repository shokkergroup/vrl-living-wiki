# SHOKKER LORE channel discovery

Use this before proposing features or a fixed archive price.

## 1. Business fit

- Who owns the show, channel, brand, and source media?
- What does the creator sell: ads, membership, events, merch, services,
  licensing, or something else?
- Who is the site primarily for: new fans, deep fans, the production team,
  sponsors, or members?
- Which outcome matters first: discovery, fandom, archive traffic, clip
  production, research, or member value?
- What would make a 30-day pilot obviously useful?

## 1b. Revenue posture and the watch-time engine

This determines the entire pitch. Most of it is answerable from the outside in
a couple of minutes; confirm the rest in the call.

- **Where do they sit on monetization?**
  - pre-monetization with a deep catalog → highest urgency, they already made
    the content and it is not being watched;
  - monetized with a dormant back catalog → highest dollar value, resurrection
    is found money;
  - monetized and thriving → lead with the world and the studio, not the money.
- Is monetization **on** for the channel, and are embeds left enabled?
- Roughly what share of views comes from uploads older than a year? (Their
  Analytics knows; ask them to look.)
- Do they have sponsors, and would a **permanent, searchable index of sponsor
  reads** be worth something at renewal time?
- Do they run a membership or Patreon that a gated archive surface could feed?
- Will they share the **Traffic source → External** report so we can cross-check
  our numbers against YouTube's?
- Any videos that must **never** be surfaced — unlisted, private, rights-limited,
  or content they have soured on?

> **Confirm out loud, and write it into the scope letter:** we never mirror
> media, playback is always the official player on their channel, 100% of ad
> revenue is theirs, and **we never promise views, watch hours, revenue, rank,
> or a timeline.** If a prospect is shopping for a guaranteed number, that is a
> disqualification, not a negotiation.

## 2. Canon boundary

- Which channels, feeds, playlists, livestreams, and bonus shows belong?
- Which uploads must be excluded?
- Are there guest appearances on other channels?
- Are there deleted, private, member-only, or rights-limited sources?
- Are continuation uploads one episode or multiple canonical events?
- What is the official source when duplicates exist?

Write an executable inclusion rule and preserve an exclusion report.

## 3. Archive reality

Capture:

- source count;
- total duration;
- publication range;
- caption/transcript coverage and quality;
- languages;
- average and maximum runtime;
- thumbnail and metadata quality;
- missing/deleted sources;
- audio-only versus video;
- speaker and guest consistency.

Sample at least ten sources across eras and formats.

## 4. The creator’s native vocabulary

- What do fans call episodes, moments, claims, evidence, eras, and corrections?
- Which generic phrases would sound fake coming from this community?
- What are the recurring segments, jokes, characters, feuds, rituals, or
  debates?
- Which subjects deserve permanent dossiers?
- Which questions do fans ask repeatedly?
- Which questions does the creator’s own team struggle to answer?
- What is sensitive, private, stale, or off-limits?

## 5. Truth and authority

- Which facts need a structured ledger rather than transcript search?
- Who can verify identities, speakers, results, claims, or canon?
- What may be public at machine, curated, editor, and creator-certified levels?
- How should corrections work?
- What must remain unknown without review?
- Who has final publishing authority?

## 6. Visual world

Do not begin with colors.

- If the archive were a physical place, what would it be?
- What objects, screens, documents, maps, tools, or rituals belong there?
- What should motion feel like?
- Which typography and textures fit the content?
- What clichés should be avoided?
- Which competitor or current brand references are useful?
- What must be recognizably present before the logo is visible?

## 7. Fan experience

Choose one strong answer for each job:

- **Find:** How should a fan ask or search?
- **Understand:** What kind of dossier or timeline matters?
- **Experience:** What can a fan play, explore, or follow?
- **Participate:** What can a fan create, compare, vote on, or share?
- **Return:** What changes weekly or monthly?

Require at least two signature interactions that would be strange on an
unrelated site.

## 8. Creator studio

- How are episodes researched now?
- Who finds clips and how long does it take?
- What makes a moment worth clipping for this creator?
- What contextual risks make a candidate unusable?
- What compilations or recurring packages would the creator make if research
  were easy?
- Who approves hooks, titles, captions, in/out points, and final exports?
- Which performance metrics can be imported after publishing?

## 9. Clip taste interview

Ask for:

- five clips they love and why;
- five clips they rejected and why;
- target platforms and runtime bands;
- profanity and advertiser constraints;
- preferred hook styles;
- faces, guests, or subjects that matter;
- visual editing rules;
- acceptable context debt;
- whether news relevance changes priority.

Turn these into visible scoring dimensions. Do not convert them into claims of
guaranteed virality.

## 10. Update operation

- publishing cadence;
- expected monthly source hours;
- desired turnaround;
- human review allowance;
- creator-certification cadence;
- correction SLA;
- clip quantity;
- hosting, analytics, backup, and export needs.

## Required proposal output

Produce:

1. channel thesis;
2. fit and risk snapshot;
3. canon and source lanes;
4. Channel DNA;
5. entity, relationship, receipt, and ledger model;
6. visual-world brief;
7. fan front doors;
8. creator tools;
9. clip opportunity model;
10. evidence and correction policy;
11. pilot scope and acceptance checks;
12. full build phases;
13. Lore Pulse plan;
14. **watch-time engine plan** — which play doors, trails, and routes ship at
    launch; whether watchalong companion mode is in scope; the Clip/SeekToAction
    rollout; and what the monthly report will contain;
15. **compliance statement** — zero autoplay, no mirrored media, public sources
    only, and the explicit note that no views, hours, revenue, rank, or timeline
    are being promised;
16. **archive band and tier**, with the caption-backed hour count that produced
    them;
17. price, assumptions, and exclusions.

> The proposal names a price. **Outreach never does.** See the outreach rules in
> §10 of the source guide.
