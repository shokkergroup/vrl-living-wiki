# SHOKKER LORE product blueprint

## The product

**Category:** creator memory world.

**One sentence:** SHOKKER LORE turns years of social content into a searchable,
playable, channel-native universe that helps fans explore the archive and helps
creators turn old material into new programming.

**Short pitch:** Your back catalog is one of your most valuable assets, but
YouTube, podcast apps, and social feeds bury it by date. SHOKKER LORE gives the
archive a memory. Fans can find any episode, person, topic, take, running joke,
or defining moment and jump to the exact source second. The creator gets the
same memory as a research desk and clip-planning system.

**Tagline:** Every episode remembered. Every moment findable. Every fan deeper
in.

“The channel remembers itself” is a strong campaign line and product promise.

**The commercial line:** *Your back catalog stops being a graveyard and starts
being a second channel.* Embedded playback counts toward the creator's public
watch hours, ads serve inside those embeds, and **only YouTube and the video
owner earn from them — the embedding site earns nothing.** We never mirror
media, so we never take revenue away. See
[WATCH_TIME_ENGINE.md](WATCH_TIME_ENGINE.md).

## What VRL and WWAM prove together

### VRL proves archive completeness and structured truth

The VRL Living Wiki has a sports-native information model:

- 222 official source broadcasts grouped into 219 canonical events;
- 486 hours of tape;
- 15 seasons and multiple broadcast eras;
- 185 reconciled identities;
- 2,539 logged moments;
- race, driver, track, result, championship, interview, excitement, and booth
  ledgers;
- Ask the Tape, Time Machine, VRL Movie, Lore Galaxy, Booth Lore, rankings,
  driver dossiers, exact-second playback, and lost-tape memorials.

Its crucial product lesson is that transcript search is not enough. “Who won?”
must route to a reviewed result ledger. “Who is this driver?” must route through
an identity registry. Continuation broadcasts must roll into one event. Unknown
champions and unverified starts must remain unknown.

### WWAM proves voice, world-building, and creator utility

The WWAM public experience presents a horror-comedy memory room rather than a
generic episode directory:

- 74 promoted sources and 177 hours in the highlighted experience;
- 872 playable receipts and 168 connected memory nodes;
- Red Band 100, WWAM Up In Ya, Ask a Character, recurring-bit bloodlines,
  source dossiers, a tape companion, fan-made memory cuts, trivia, time
  capsules, and a verdict room;
- creator-facing clip candidates, supercuts, resurfacing pairs, campaign
  packets, taste calibration, intake quarantine, review queues, and pilot
  briefs;
- an underlying canonical registry broader than the promoted memory-ready set.

Its crucial product lesson is that channel personality is not decoration.
Vocabulary, humor model, recurring characters, interaction design, and visual
world all belong in the Channel DNA. Its second lesson is that machine findings,
human curation, authenticated review, and creator certification are different
states.

### The combined proof

VRL looks like a race-control archive. WWAM looks like an illicit horror
evidence room. They can still share source, receipt, entity, relationship,
dossier, search, playback, correction, and review contracts.

That is SHOKKER LORE:

> Reuse the memory spine. Re-author the world.

## What is commercially ready and what is not

### Ready now

- two radically different, live proof worlds;
- complete-archive ingestion and exact-second source playback;
- strong canon, identity, evidence, correction, and validation thinking;
- reusable ChannelPack and Memory OS contracts;
- fan discovery, search, dossier, timeline, ranking, companion, game, and
  playable-path patterns;
- creator clip, resurfacing, taste, intake, review, and pilot patterns;
- a credible paid proof-pilot offer.

### Needs product hardening before scale

- consolidate the reusable engines and schemas into one maintained core rather
  than letting universal logic drift across client projects;
- build authenticated owner/editor roles for production review and
  certification;
- add a standard analytics event contract for archive search, source play,
  clip adoption, and return behavior;
- establish client agreements for brand rights, source media, clips, quotes,
  data export, hosting, corrections, and takedowns;
- reduce feature sprawl by choosing a small number of clear front doors for
  each client;
- document real pilot outcomes so the sales case proves business value, not
  only technical and creative possibility.

The demos prove capability. The first outside pilots must prove repeatable
delivery, buyer value, review cost, and recurring willingness to pay.

## The six product layers

### 1. LORE CORE — the durable memory

The invisible, reusable foundation:

- canonical source registry;
- explicit inclusion, exclusion, and exception rules;
- metadata, transcripts/captions, thumbnails, durations, and availability;
- exact-second receipts with bounded context;
- canonical entities and aliases;
- evidence-backed relationships;
- per-source dossiers;
- topic, person, event, claim, joke, and chronology indexes;
- corrections, tombstones, evidence tiers, and coverage gaps;
- deterministic rebuilds and validation.

LORE CORE is what compounds. A new upload adds more than a page: it can update
entities, relationships, timelines, claims, recurring bits, clip candidates,
and fan paths.

### 2. CHANNEL DNA — what makes one creator irreplaceable

Every client pack defines:

- the canon boundary and source lanes;
- people, subjects, events, recurring formats, aliases, and relationships;
- what counts as a meaningful moment;
- the creator’s editorial voice and forbidden generic language;
- native labels for receipts, unknowns, review, search, and corrections;
- scoring dimensions and their visible weights;
- clip-worthy signals and contextual risks;
- visual metaphor, typography, color, texture, motion, and sound policy;
- signature fan rituals and creator workflows.

Channel DNA is configuration and editorial knowledge, not proof. A pack can say
“look for a prediction” or “look for a photo finish”; the resulting public item
still needs a source receipt.

### 3. LORE WORLD — the fan experience

Every site should provide four universal jobs while expressing them in
channel-native ways:

| Fan job | Universal behavior | Possible native expression |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Find | Search sources, entities, moments, and exact transcript evidence | Ask the Tape, Search the Territory, Ask the Newsroom |
| Understand | Recaps, dossiers, timelines, relationships, and context | Driver File, Wrestler Dossier, Model Brief |
| Experience | Playable paths, synchronized companions, maps, games, and stories | VRL Movie, Territory Map, Launch Timeline |
| Participate | Share receipts, build cuts, vote, play trivia, create lists | Fantasy Card, Fan Cut, Receipt Battle |

Every build must ship:

- a complete archive door;
- a search or question door;
- entity/topic dossiers;
- exact-source playback;
- one signature discovery mechanic;
- one signature participatory mechanic;
- visible evidence and correction language.

### 4. LORE STUDIO — value returned to the creator

The same archive powers:

- source research and episode prep;
- “what have we said about this?” lookup;
- recurring-topic and take timelines;
- guest, character, franchise, driver, company, or product dossiers;
- hot-moment candidates with exact source windows;
- themed supercuts across multiple uploads;
- old/new resurfacing pairs;
- cold-open and compilation briefs;
- clip manifests for editors;
- owner/editor review queues;
- performance feedback without mutating historical truth.

LORE STUDIO should be sold as a workflow, not as an unsupported promise that a
machine can predict virality or finish an edit without review.

### 5. LORE PULSE — the recurring service

For each new upload:

1. discover it from an approved source;
2. hold it in intake quarantine;
3. acquire captions or preserve the missing-evidence state;
4. distill topics, entities, moments, quotes, claims, and native signals;
5. connect it to the existing memory;
6. create a source dossier, recap, and clip shortlist;
7. review high-impact candidates;
8. publish the approved update;
9. report what changed in the archive.

This makes the monthly service tangible: the client is not paying for
“maintenance.” They are paying to keep the memory alive.

### 6. LORE RETURN — the value that flows back to the channel

The layer that separates this from every organizing tool in the category. The
archive is not a destination that competes with the channel; it is a machine
that points deliberate human attention *at* the channel.

- exact-second play doors, every one a counted view when a human clicks it;
- a 30-second floor enforced in the receipt compiler so every door can actually
  earn a view;
- playable trails, “Start Here” routes, and watchalong companion mode, which
  produces full-length views;
- `VideoObject` + `Clip` + `SeekToAction` structured data generated directly
  from the moment ledger, making the creator's videos eligible for
  jump-to-second Key Moments in Google Search;
- a sponsor and segment index that turns the archive into a sponsor asset;
- back-catalog resurrection, giving dormant uploads a reason to be watched;
- first-party play instrumentation and a monthly report the creator can verify
  against their own YouTube analytics.

**Governing principle: manufacture intent, never manufacture plays.** No
autoplay, no hidden players, no auto-advance, no incentivized viewing. A play
without intent does not count *and* endangers the client's channel. The full
compliance spine is in [WATCH_TIME_ENGINE.md](WATCH_TIME_ENGINE.md).

**Why the recurring plan is permanent:** Partner Program watch-hour
requirements run on a rolling twelve-month window. Watch hours are a treadmill,
not a finish line. An archive that keeps producing deliberate plays every month
stays valuable indefinitely.

## The uniqueness system

Do not standardize finished pages. Standardize the questions that generate the
pages.

### The 60 / 25 / 15 rule

- **60% reusable core:** ingestion, evidence, identity, search, dossier,
  playback, correction, review, and deployment contracts.
- **25% Channel DNA:** taxonomy, scoring, voice, labels, editorial rules,
  clip signals, and content lanes.
- **15% signature invention:** art direction and interactions that should not
  make sense for another client.

The visible result can feel 100% bespoke even when most reliability work is
reused invisibly.

### Five uniqueness gates

A client does not launch until it has:

1. a native world metaphor;
2. at least two signature interactions;
3. one creator workflow built around how that show actually works;
4. one fan ritual worth returning for;
5. no copied vocabulary, score dimensions, or feature names from another
   client unless the concept is intentionally universal.

## New-vertical concept: wrestling talk and analysis

The site should not be “WWAM with wrestling nouns.”

### Information model

- host, guest, wrestler, promoter, booker;
- promotion, territory, event, match, angle, storyline, title;
- analysis, story, prediction, reaction, correction, lesson, recurring bit;
- date, era, source, receipt, relationship.

### Fan front doors

- **The Complete Tape Library:** every episode, indexed by wrestler, promotion,
  era, event, and subject.
- **Angle Atlas:** an interactive graph of the matches, personalities, and
  storylines discussed across the show.
- **Take Timeline:** how the show’s documented analysis of a person or angle
  developed over time, using cautious evidence language.
- **Tape Study:** playable breakdowns of psychology, safety, performance, or
  storytelling when those are genuine recurring show subjects.
- **Build a Card:** fans assemble a fantasy card from subjects in the archive
  and receive playable supporting receipts.
- **Receipt Battle:** two takes or eras, side by side, with exact source clips.

### Creator tools

- named-wrestler research packets before recording;
- prior-take lookup to avoid accidental repetition or create intentional
  callbacks;
- backstage-story and teaching-moment clip queues;
- themed supercuts by wrestler, promotion, event, or booking concept;
- “what became newly relevant this week?” resurfacing against current events.

### Wrestling clip signals

- a decisive or surprising claim;
- a named star or major event in the opening seconds;
- a self-contained backstage story;
- disagreement or reversal;
- an emotional or comic turn;
- a practical teaching point;
- a clean setup and payoff;
- renewed relevance because of current wrestling news.

The score should rank editorial usefulness. The editor still verifies context,
rights, speaker identity, in/out points, and current relevance.

## New-vertical concept: AI news and analysis

### Information model

- company, model, product, researcher, paper, benchmark, law, regulator;
- launch, claim, prediction, demo, comparison, correction, tutorial;
- capability, limitation, price, availability, safety, business impact;
- date, version, source, receipt, relationship.

### Fan front doors

- **What Changed This Week:** a source-backed change log across the indexed
  shows.
- **Model Time Machine:** every documented claim about one model or company in
  chronological order.
- **Claim Watch:** predictions and strong claims with later follow-up evidence;
  unresolved claims remain unresolved.
- **Launch Room:** one dossier per major launch, linking every episode,
  explanation, reaction, and correction.
- **Ask the Briefing Room:** answer archive questions from structured launch,
  claim, and model ledgers before falling back to transcript search.
- **Explain It to Me:** playable learning paths from beginner to advanced.

### Creator tools

- pre-show company/model briefing packs;
- contradiction and correction alerts;
- resurfacing of old predictions when a new launch occurs;
- clip candidates for concise explanations, contrarian claims, comparisons,
  and consequential forecasts;
- episode gap analysis: important active entities not covered recently.

### AI-news clip signals

- time sensitivity;
- novelty;
- consequence for a clear audience;
- named company/model/product;
- strong standalone explanation;
- disagreement with conventional wisdom;
- a testable prediction;
- compact setup and payoff;
- low context debt.

Timeliness must be rechecked at editorial time. The archive timestamp proves
what the host said then; it does not prove that a fast-changing fact remains
current.

## Clip opportunity engine

### What it should produce

For each candidate:

- official source and exact timestamp;
- proposed in/out window;
- bounded transcript excerpt;
- why it surfaced;
- channel-specific component score;
- contextual and rights risk;
- suggested hook, title, caption, and visual treatment clearly labeled as new
  promotional copy;
- status: machine candidate, curated candidate, editor verified, approved,
  rejected, or published;
- optional post-performance metrics.

### What it must never claim

- guaranteed virality;
- a speaker identity inferred only from captions;
- rights clearance;
- creator approval without an authenticated decision;
- “first ever” or “changed their mind” without the required evidence;
- that a proposed title or hook is an archival quote.

### The learning loop

Learn separately from:

1. **editor choice:** selected, rejected, needs context;
2. **edit outcome:** final runtime, hook style, subject, platform;
3. **performance:** views, retention, completion, shares, saves, comments;
4. **business result:** subscribers, member conversions, sponsor value when the
   client can actually measure them.

Editorial taste may reorder future candidates. It may not rewrite receipts,
evidence, historical scores, or canon.

## Best buyer profile

Prioritize creators with:

- at least 50 long-form uploads or 100 hours of meaningful archive;
- recurring people, subjects, formats, jokes, predictions, stories, or events;
- a fanbase that references old episodes;
- regular new publishing;
- revenue from sponsors, memberships, live events, products, or consulting;
- no good internal way to answer “when did we talk about that?”;
- authority to use their brand and create excerpts or clips.

Also score their **monetization posture** — it changes the pitch entirely:

- **pre-monetization with a deep catalog** — highest urgency; they already made
  the content and it is not being watched (never mention thresholds or promise
  to cross one);
- **monetized with a dormant back catalog** — highest dollar value; resurrection
  is found money;
- **monetized and thriving** — lead with the world and the creator studio, not
  the money.

Strong initial verticals:

- wrestling, combat sports, racing, and sports analysis;
- horror, film, television, and pop-culture shows;
- AI, technology, finance, and news analysis;
- interview and personality-led podcasts;
- gaming communities and actual-play shows;
- education channels with a deep evergreen library.

Verticals no feed-based competitor can reach — all have deep video, no RSS, and
a motivated owner:

- **true crime** — exceptional fit; entity-dense, detail-obsessed audience, and
  the subject matter demands exactly the evidence tiers and refused answers
  nobody else has;
- **faith and sermon archives** — decades of video, congregations genuinely
  search for what was said about a topic or passage, institutional budget and
  continuity, zero competitors;
- **local and school sports broadcasts** — boosters, parents, and alumni are
  motivated buyers with real budgets and no alternative;
- **civic and municipal video** — public record with legal retention, huge
  archives, journalists and citizens who need search;
- **conference and talk archives**, **outdoors and sponsor-driven channels**,
  **car builds and restoration series**.

Avoid leading with creators who have only short-form posts, unclear rights,
little recurring lore, no active publishing cadence, or an expectation of a
$29/month template.

## Delivery workflow

### Phase 0 — fast fit check

- inventory sources and archive hours;
- sample ten representative uploads;
- identify evidence quality and rights constraints;
- interview the creator or expert;
- choose the closest fixed package and identify any work that must become an
  add-on.

Do this quickly for productized $250–$1,500 clients. Reserve a separate paid
discovery engagement for custom networks, mixed-rights archives, or unusual
integrations.

### Phase 1 — affordable proof build

Use a $250 league build or $500 creator pilot. Ship:

- the searchable usable public catalog from one primary channel or feed;
- one entity/topic dossier;
- one fan signature interaction;
- one creator tool, usually a clip or research queue;
- a trust/coverage report;
- a full-archive proposal with observed—not invented—pilot results.

### Phase 2 — archive build

- lock canon and source lanes;
- ingest in reviewable batches;
- reconcile identities and aliases;
- build ledgers and channel-native derivations;
- implement the visual world and signature mechanics;
- test benchmark questions and failure cases;
- launch with visible coverage and correction routes.

### Phase 3 — Lore Pulse

- process new uploads;
- update connected memory;
- deliver clip/research packets;
- review corrections and stale sources;
- publish a monthly “what the archive learned” report.

## Trust is part of the product

Every public factual or editorial item should answer:

> Which official source, at what time, supports this?

Use four distinct evidence states:

1. machine surfaced;
2. timestamp-validated human-curated candidate;
3. authenticated editor verified;
4. creator certified.

Unknown is a valid result. Corrections are append-only. Contradictory evidence
survives. Removed media leaves a tombstone. Scores name their components.
Fan-made cuts and generated copy remain separate from archival evidence.

This is not merely defensive. Trust makes the archive useful to the creator,
not only entertaining to fans.

## Sales language

### 30-second pitch

You already paid to create hundreds of hours of content, but almost all of its
value disappears into a reverse-chronological feed. SHOKKER LORE turns that
archive into a living world. Fans can search every episode, explore people and
stories, build playable paths, and jump to the exact moment. Your team gets a
research and clip studio built from the same source-backed memory. It looks and
behaves like your show because we model your show’s actual DNA.

### 30-second pitch, revenue version

Use this when the prospect has a monetization or dormant-catalog problem.

Right now your old episodes are buried by date and nobody finds them. We turn
the whole archive into a world fans explore, and every moment they explore is a
play button pointed at your channel. Embedded plays count toward your watch
hours, ads run on them, and every cent goes to you. We don’t host your video, we
don’t take a cut, and every month you get a report plus instructions for
checking it against YouTube’s own analytics.

### The three sentences that beat a $19/month tool

1. Their product makes your archive searchable. Ours makes it pay.
2. We never copy your media. Every play is on your channel, with your ads, and
   we earn nothing from it.
3. Every month you get a report, and instructions for verifying it against
   YouTube's own analytics.

### The pain

- YouTube knows uploads; it does not know the show’s lore.
- Search cannot reliably answer structured questions.
- New fans do not know where to start.
- Old episodes stop earning attention.
- Clip editors repeatedly hunt through the same tape.
- Creators forget earlier takes, stories, and callbacks.
- Generic AI summaries flatten the personality that created the fandom.

### The promise

SHOKKER LORE makes the whole catalog:

- findable;
- playable;
- connected;
- useful for new content;
- unmistakably native to the creator.

### The moat

The moat is not code generation. It is the compounding combination of:

- a rights posture that never copies media, so playback always happens on the
  creator's own channel and 100% of the ad revenue stays theirs;
- a moment ledger that maps one-to-one onto Google's Clip markup, which no
  transcript-search tool can produce;
- a clean canonical archive;
- exact-source receipts;
- reconciled identities and relationships;
- corrections and human decisions;
- channel-specific editorial models;
- creator taste and performance feedback;
- a fan experience whose interaction language belongs to that community.

## Product boundaries

Do not promise:

- **any number of views, watch hours, dollars, or subscribers**, or any
  timeline for reaching one;
- **that we can get a channel monetized** or across any threshold;
- **anything implying influence over the YouTube algorithm**, or anything that
  sounds like buying traffic;
- guaranteed traffic, SEO rank, retention, revenue, or virality;
- automatic rights clearance;
- perfect captions or speaker diarization;
- factual certainty from a title or transcript fragment;
- fully automatic public publishing without a review agreement.

Sell what can be demonstrated:

- archive coverage;
- search and benchmark answer quality;
- exact-source playback;
- dossiers and connections;
- reviewable clip opportunities;
- time saved in a measured pilot;
- increased archive discovery measured after launch.

## Immediate commercialization plan

1. Package VRL and WWAM as two opposite case studies.
2. Sell a $250 current-season package to small racing leagues.
3. Sell $500 creator pilots, then offer $1,000 and $1,500 expansion packages.
4. Attach a $25, $50, or $100 monthly update plan to every build.
5. Require permission to measure archive clicks, source play-through, searches,
   clip adoption, and creator time saved.
6. Use archive expansions, signature modules, reviewed ledgers, and clip packs

   and tool costs.
