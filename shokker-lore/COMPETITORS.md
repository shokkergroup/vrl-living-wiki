# The field — who else is doing this

Researched July 25, 2026. Prices are what each vendor published at that date;
re-check before quoting against them.

**Headline finding:** the category is real and already contested — but almost
entirely by **self-serve templates**. Nobody found is selling an authored,
creator-owned memory world as a done-for-you service. The gap SHOKKER LORE
occupies is genuinely open. It is not, however, an empty field, and a prospect
who opens a search tab will find the tools below in about thirty seconds.

---

## 1. PodRewind — the closest thing to a direct competitor

<https://podrewind.com> · <https://podrewind.com/pricing>

Self-serve SaaS. Tagline: *"Your Podcast Archive, Unlocked."* Explicitly sells
a **public wiki** for the audience.

| Tier | Price | Includes |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Free | $0 | 5 episodes, basic transcription, search, speaker detection, 20 chat messages, 5-episode public wiki |
| Creator | $19/mo | Unlimited episodes, auto-transcription, unlimited chat, 100 content credits, 1 seat, custom domain access, wiki show notes |
| Pro | $49/mo | Hosted wiki on custom domain, TikTok-ready clipping, auto show notes, unlimited content credits, unlimited seats, **6 wiki layout options**, "Powered by" badge removal, website theme matching |

Also: 500 hours/month included (1,000 on annual), **$0.20/hour** beyond that
for bulk back-catalog import. Non-profit discounts. Claims 95%+ transcription
accuracy across 100+ languages.

**Why this matters:** a two-year Pilot-tier client at $600 + $99/mo pays
$2,976. PodRewind Pro over the same window is $1,176. That gap has to be
earned out loud, not assumed.

**What they cannot do:**

- **RSS-shaped only.** No feed, no product. Sim-racing leagues, YouTube-only
  shows, watchalong channels, and actual-play campaigns are structurally
  unreachable.
- **Six layouts is still a template.** Every customer's wiki is a sibling of
  every other customer's wiki.
- No structured ledgers, identity registry, evidence tiers, correction memory,
  refused-answer states, fan rituals, or creator research desk.
- No human in the loop. Nobody to call when the archive is wrong.

**Also worth noting:** podrewind.com runs a large SEO content operation
(`/tools/<competitor>` review pages for Podbean, Acast, Libsyn, Buzzsprout,
Cleanfeed, and others, plus a blog). They are playing a search-distribution
game, not a craft game. That is both a warning — they will out-rank us — and a
signal that they are optimizing for volume signups, not depth per client.

## 2. PodSearch

<https://www.podsearch.tech> · *"Turn Dead Podcast Episodes Into a Searchable
AI Database."*

Transcribes, indexes, and hosts an AI search engine over a back catalog.
Pricing and feature detail were **not extractable from the public page** at the
time of research — the site is thin. Treat as an early or low-maturity
competitor, but re-check: the positioning line is almost identical to ours.

## 3. Repurposing tools — adjacent, own the clip layer

| Tool | Price | What it does |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Castmagic | ~$19/mo annual (~$39 monthly) | One recording → 40+ assets: show notes, blog posts, social, newsletter, pull quotes, YouTube descriptions |
| Opus Clip | $15/mo (150 credits), $29/mo Pro | Automated vertical clipping; 1 credit = 1 minute uploaded |
| Descript | ~$16–$24/mo | Full text-based audio/video editor with transcription |
| Podium | — | Fast show notes, summaries, timestamped highlights |

**All episode-scoped.** None of them know what the show said three years ago,
so none can surface a callback, a contradiction, a resurfacing pair, or a
"first time they discussed X." That is the entire creator-side wedge.

## 4. Podcast intelligence — not competitors

- **Listen Notes** — podcast search engine and API; claims 178M+ episodes
  across 3M+ podcasts.
- **Podscan.fm** — transcribes and indexes millions of episodes for real-time
  brand monitoring: keyword mentions with transcript snippet, sentiment,
  AI-verification flag, and timestamp.
- **Podcast Index** — open, free directory and API.

These index *everyone* shallowly and sell to brands, agencies, and developers.
They never appear on the creator's domain and are not the creator's product.
Useful as prospecting infrastructure, not as rivals.

## 5. Fan wiki platforms — the incumbent nobody likes

**Fandom** is the default home for fan wikis and the reason "wiki" carries
baggage. Communities are actively migrating away over aggressive advertising —
scam-adjacent link ads, autoplaying video, full-screen popups obscuring
content — plus degraded functionality and loss of editorial control.

Documented migrations include the **Minecraft** wiki (voted for a full exit,
moved to Weird Gloop), **Godzilla**, and **Hollow Knight**. Tools exist purely
to route around Fandom: *Indie Wiki Buddy* (redirects to independent wikis) and
*BreezeWiki* (an ad-free Fandom mirror).

Alternatives: **Miraheze** (free, open source, widely recommended),
**ShoutWiki**, self-hosted **MediaWiki**.

**This is the most important finding in the document.** The entire fan-wiki
category is volunteer-run, ad-infested, and not owned by the creator. Nobody is
selling a paid, beautiful, official, creator-owned alternative. Fandom's SEO
authority is enormous and not worth fighting head-on — but "your fans deserve
better than Fandom, and you should own it" is a real pitch to a real grievance.

## 6. Consumer fandom search — name collision, not competition

A venture-funded startup named **Lore** (~$1.1M pre-seed led by Initialized
Capital, with backing from Elad Gil, Balaji Srinivasan, and executives at
OpenAI and Figma) is building an **AI search engine for fandoms**, aimed at
fans rather than creators: a personalized graph of obsessions, a fandom
updates feed, monthly reports, and deep dives into niche topics. Founded by
Zehra Naqvi (previously co-founded Quilt, acquired by Asana in 2022). Came out
of stealth in October 2025 with 24,000+ early-tester searches; invite-only with
a broader rollout planned for early 2026.

Not a competitor — opposite side of the market. But **flag the name
collision**: they are well-funded, getting press, and own the "lore + fandom +
search" association in tech coverage. Consider how "SHOKKER LORE" reads in a
search result next to them, and whether the Shokker prefix is doing enough
work. This is a branding question, not a product one, and it does not need
answering today.

## The four wedges

### 1. Video-native, not feed-native

The template tools are built around an RSS feed. A large share of the best
prospects — sim-racing leagues, YouTube-only shows, watchalong channels,
actual-play campaigns, gaming communities — have no feed. Both live proof
worlds (VRL, WWAM) are video archives. This is the widest, cleanest gap.

### 2. Structured truth, not retrieval

*"Who won the 2019 finale?"* is a ledger question. Transcript retrieval answers
it confidently and wrongly. Reviewed ledgers, identity registries, alias
quarantine, evidence tiers, and refused-answer states are the difference
between a demo and an archive a creator will publicly stand behind. No
self-serve tool has any of this, and none can — it requires human judgment per
client.

### 3. Authored world, not layout picker

Six layouts is still a template. The no-reskin rule is the whole product: a
world whose vocabulary, metaphor, and rituals would look absurd on any other
channel. That cannot be shipped by a signup form. It is also the reason we can
charge more — and the reason we must actually do it every time.

### 4. The archive earns again

**The strongest wedge, and the newest.** Every tool in this document sells
*organization*. None of them sell *revenue*.

Embedded playback counts toward the creator's public watch hours — including
the 4,000-hour monetization threshold — and ads serve inside embedded players.
Critically, **only YouTube and the video owner earn from those ads; the
embedding site earns nothing.** So we can say, literally and without hedging:
*we take no cut, we never copy your media, and every play is on your channel
with your ads.*

Any competitor who caches or mirrors audio is quietly taking that revenue away
from the creator. We can name that difference out loud.

And the mechanism that makes it work is one nobody else has: our **moment
ledger maps one-to-one onto Google's `Clip` markup**, so the creator's videos
can surface in Search with jump-to-second Key Moments. Curated, named, bounded
moments are the input — and no self-serve transcript tool produces them.

See [WATCH_TIME_ENGINE.md](WATCH_TIME_ENGINE.md) for the full system, the
compliance spine, and the monthly report.

## The objection you will actually hear

> *"I found something that does this for twenty bucks a month."*

Do not argue the feature list — the feature list looks similar and arguing it
concedes the frame. Answer with their own archive:

1. Show the structured question their tool answers **wrongly**.
2. Show the moment their tool cannot play at the **exact second**.
3. Show the fan ritual that only makes sense for **their** show.
4. Name what they will never get from a signup form: their world, their words,
   their canon, on their domain, with a human who corrects it.

**If the honest answer is that the cheap tool is enough for that prospect, say
so and walk.** A shallow archive is a bad client and a worse case study.

## Positioning ladder

Use the highest rung the prospect will accept.

1. *"Searchable archive."* — Weakest. Commoditized. Never lead here.
2. *"The tools make an archive searchable. SHOKKER LORE makes it mean
   something — and hands the creator back a production desk built from it."*
3. *"Your back catalog stops being a graveyard and starts being a second
   channel."* — **Strongest.** Ties the world to money, and it is verifiable
   against the client's own YouTube analytics.
